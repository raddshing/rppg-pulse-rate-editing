Each entry is dated and committed before any test data are opened. The original document, the original manifest and earlier entries are not rewritten.

- 2026-11-11: Added docs/PREREGISTRATION_STAGE5_MANIFEST_A1.csv with the SHA-256 of the two RhythmFormer (i-pc) checkpoints (seeds 20261132 and 20261133) that finished training after the original commit.
- 2026-11-11: Added the October modules imported by the code added in commit dbb4535 (src.st3.c4_description, src.st3.media, src.st3.progress, src.st3.r3, src.st3.waves, src.st5c.avi_packets, src.st5c.base, src.st5c.signals), unchanged from commit 6ebbc9cd. The evaluation is not changed.
- 2026-11-11: Clarification of Section 5. Both key-frame exclusion rules are applied to the standard HR readout of Section 3, and only the listed bins are set to zero. The October code for this analysis also removed the window mean before the periodogram. This is not part of either rule; its effect on the development half is reported in the paper.
- 2026-11-11: Added src/st3/base.py, unchanged from commit 6ebbc9cd. Its embedded subject list equals the A split in lists/mmpd_subjects_A_B.csv. The evaluation is not changed.
- 2026-11-11: Added a sensitivity analysis to Section 5, fixed on MMPD-A (development data). On MMPD-B, recordings whose background shows a periodic peak near 2.0 Hz or 3.0 Hz are excluded, and C1–C4 are repeated. The background signal is the mean of the R, G and B values over the pixels outside the face box in each frame. The face box is the one used for the PhysNet input view: a fixed OpenCV detection on the first frame, enlarged as for that view and clipped to the frame. The spectrum is a Welch estimate on the 512-point HR grid at 30 fps: linear detrend of the whole signal, Hann window, 300-sample segments with 150-sample overlap, segment means removed. A recording is flagged when this spectrum has, in bins 33–35 (116.02–123.05 bpm) or 50–52 (175.78–182.81 bpm), a bin that is larger than both neighbours and at least twice the median of the six surrounding bins, three on each side. Recordings without background pixels are left out of this analysis. The implementation is src/stage5/mmpd_background_flags.py.
- 2026-11-13: Inference batching, random numbers and two sensitivity analyses. The evaluation of Sections 3 and 4 is not changed.
  1. Batching. In the inference code used for all arms, FactorizePhys outputs are standardized with the mean and standard deviation of the whole inference batch, as in the rPPG-Toolbox FactorizePhys test code (commit b7500b8). With FSAM enabled, its BVP head also uses the batch-wide minimum. FactorizePhys outputs therefore depend slightly on which chunks share a batch: in a development check on 30 MMPD-A windows, changing the batching changed the standardized waveform by up to 0.40 and the narrow-band HR readout of one window. PhysNet and RhythmFormer waveforms changed by at most 0.044, with no change in HR. The batching is fixed for all backbones and arms. MMPD-B: the chunks of the October input plan in its full order, four chunks per batch; batches may cross recording boundaries, and the last batch holds the remaining chunks. This is the batching used for MMPD-A, and the comparison of the re-inferred original-arm predictions with the stored October predictions (Section 3, Run) also verifies it. MCD-rPPG test half: per recording, chunks in temporal order, four chunks per batch; the last batch of a recording holds the remaining chunks. This is the batching used for the development half.
  2. Random numbers. The per-recording reset of the random-number state stays in place. In this code revision inference uses no random numbers: the FSAM basis is initialized with ones and dropout is inactive at evaluation. The reset therefore has no effect.
  3. FSAM. For FactorizePhys, the confirmatory contrasts are also reported with FSAM disabled at inference, as a sensitivity analysis.
  4. Line-removal input. No line-removal input procedure is fixed, so this sensitivity analysis of Section 5 is omitted. On the development half, RhythmFormer was re-inferred after removing, at each pixel, the mean deviation of each of the 12 frame phases of the key-frame interval from that pixel's overall mean. The key-frame-line fraction of high-HR windows fell by 0.012 to 0.071 across arms, and the contrasts changed by at most 0.53 bpm in high-HR MAE and by at most 1.12 bpm in low-HR MAE. This is reported as an exploratory result.
- 2026-11-15: Exploratory arms, test-run protocol, reporting scope and pre-written statements, fixed before the test data are opened for Stage 5. Sections 3 and 4, the confirmatory contrasts C1–C4 and the earlier entries are not changed, with one exception stated in item 7c. No arm is added to the test run after this date. Development-only diagnostics, which are never evaluated on the test sets, may be completed after this date; item 9 lists them.
  1. FactorizePhys settings and line removal (clarifying the 2026-11-13 entry). The primary analysis of FactorizePhys uses FSAM enabled at inference, as in the October predictions; FSAM disabled is a sensitivity analysis. As a further sensitivity analysis, the FactorizePhys confirmatory contrasts are recomputed from predictions made with one chunk per batch. For this analysis and for FSAM disabled, the largest absolute change of each contrast is reported, and a contrast is described as unchanged only if it keeps its tier of item 7. Line removal. The sensitivity analysis remains omitted, because neither the procedure of the 2026-11-13 entry nor Gaussian low-pass filtering (rule 2 of docs/STAGE5_DEV_DIAGNOSTICS.md) removed most of the key-frame line on the development half. The two key-frame exclusion rules, the key-frame-line fraction and the narrow band of Section 5 are unchanged.
  2. Exploratory arms. The checkpoints listed in docs/PREREGISTRATION_STAGE5_MANIFEST_A4.csv are evaluated in the same run and under the same settings as the confirmatory arms. They are: time resampling of video and label together without label shift, (i-simple), and the rate-dependent misalignment arm (ii-lag), injection −g*(t − τ − T*/2), for the three backbones; for PhysNet and FactorizePhys, the label-distribution and processing arms (ii-pc-g) (targets in 100–120 bpm moved by +20 bpm), (ii-pc-g80) (targets below 100 bpm mapped to 70 + (h* − 70)/3), (ii-pc-g2) (both maps) and (sham-pc) (identity edit, h* = h0, original label); and, for PhysNet only, (i-pc-g80) (time resampling with the targets of (ii-pc-g80)) and (roi-pc) (suppression and injection restricted to forehead and cheek regions). All edited arms except (ii-lag) use the injection −g*(t − d) of (ii-pc). Their implementation is that of the repository at commit 19ecf5d, with machine-specific paths redacted as stated in item 4. All results of these arms are exploratory. Arms and contrasts marked "values only" in item 3 carry no statement. A checkpoint is treated as defective only for a failure that is established without reference to results (its hash differs from the manifest, it cannot be loaded, or its outputs are non-finite in more than half of the windows of a test set) and only before stage (c). Its arm is then dropped from the affected analyses and this is reported; it is not retrained. A checkpoint file that is restored from a copy with the manifest hash is not defective.
  3. Named exploratory contrasts, reported in full whatever their outcome, per backbone, paired by seed, on high-HR MAE unless stated:
     - (i-pc) − (0) and (i-simple) − (0), aligned time resampling against no augmentation, by tier; (i) − (i-simple), the cost of the τ-rule label shift of (i) relative to resampling without a shift, by tier; (i) − (0), (ii) − (0) and (ii) − (i-simple), values only.
     - (ii-pc) − (i-simple) and (i-simple) − (i-pc). The label shift of (i-pc), d(r − 1), keeps the natural lag d and is called the lag-preserving shift. (i-simple) and (i-pc) are called equivalent for a backbone only if the 95% interval of (i-simple) − (i-pc) lies within ±3.52 bpm on both test sets. If the interval includes 0 but is wider, we state that no difference was established and give the interval. If the interval excludes 0, we state which of the two had the lower high-HR error on that test set and give the interval. These statements are not exclusive: if the interval excludes 0 on a test set and the equivalence condition is met, the difference is reported as established but smaller than one bin of the HR grid (3.52 bpm).
     - (ii-lag) − (ii-pc) and (ii-lag) − (ii), the mechanism contrasts; primary for PhysNet and FactorizePhys, values only for RhythmFormer.
     - Label contrasts, PhysNet primary and FactorizePhys values only: (ii-pc-g80) − (ii-pc), (ii-pc-g2) − (ii-pc-g), (ii-pc-g) − (ii-pc) and the interaction [(ii-pc-g2) − (ii-pc-g)] − [(ii-pc-g80) − (ii-pc)]; (i-pc-g80) − (i-pc), values only. Each is also given by reference-HR bin (70–80, 80–90, 90–100, 100–110, 110–120 and ≥ 120 bpm) for MAE and signed error, with window and subject counts; the bins are not merged or redefined.
     - Processing contrasts, PhysNet primary and FactorizePhys values only: (sham-pc) − (0), for low- and high-HR MAE separately; (ii-pc) − (sham-pc); (roi-pc) − (ii-pc); (roi-pc) − (0), values only.
  4. Test-run protocol. The test run uses the evaluation script at commit 19ecf5d, which reproduced the development results before this entry, gave identical prediction files when run twice on the same development inputs under the guard below, and gave the same confirmatory results when the predictions of one exploratory checkpoint were left out. The code is published at commit 19ecf5d; in the files listed in docs/A4_REDACTIONS.csv, values that are machine-specific paths are replaced by placeholders. docs/A4_CODE_SHA256.txt gives the SHA-256 of every code file as run, and docs/A4_CODE_SHA256_THIRD_PARTY.txt that of the third-party rPPG-Toolbox files; before each stage the files to be run are checked against these lists. The test run has three stages. Each stage is started by the command recorded in eval5/drive/step.sh at commit 19ecf5d, which takes no arguments other than those fixed there (expected recording count of MMPD-B: 435, from 22 subjects, as in the October code); stage (b) is started only through run_test_b_locks.sh at the same commit, which holds the locks of both GPUs until the stage has ended. Stage (b) runs on GPUs that no other job uses until stage (b) has ended, with the GPU model and software environment of the rehearsal. The allocator's out-of-memory count is recorded for every checkpoint, and stage (b) stops and is repeated from its start if an out-of-memory event is reported, because the convolution library then silently changes its computation plan and with it the outputs. Face detection runs in every stage on a single CPU thread, with OpenCL disabled in OpenCV and the GPU hidden from the detection processes, so that neither the order of two equally large detections nor the device on which the detector runs can change between runs; the number of test recordings whose first frame has two or more equally large detections is reported. The SHA-256 hashes of the outputs of each stage are committed, and an OpenTimestamps proof of the hash file is created, before the next stage starts. The proof is upgraded to a Bitcoin attestation when it becomes available, and the hash file and the proof files are kept in the repository. The commits are pushed to the remote repository before the next stage starts; stage (a) does not start until a push of this entry has succeeded.
     (a) the window lists, reference HRs, high-HR flags, background flags (2026-11-11 entry) and bootstrap index sets of MMPD-B and of the MCD-rPPG test half, generated from the raw data by the October code (for MMPD-B, the October modules st11x.future.mmpd_b_references and pathways.mmpd_b_membership, with src/st3/c4.py, as of commit dbb4535, with only file paths changed and the expected recording count made a parameter; for the MCD-rPPG test half, the code used for the development half, including the recording selection of Section 3), and the frame rate and key-frame interval of each MCD-rPPG test recording, read from the bitstream headers without decoding pixels. Stage (a) also records the SHA-256 of the MMPD-B inference input plan and of the stored October predictions of the original arms on both test sets, which are taken unchanged from the October evaluation; these predictions are read only for the comparison between stages (b) and (c). The MMPD-B window list and reference HRs are compared with the sets of the October evaluation; if they are equal, this is stated with the hash of the October files. If they differ, the cause is established and reported before stage (b): an error of the regeneration is corrected, stage (a) is repeated and both sets of hashes are reported; otherwise the regenerated sets are used, and the confirmatory contrasts are also given on the October sets.
     (b) the saved predictions of every checkpoint in the manifests, confirmatory checkpoints first, including the FactorizePhys predictions with FSAM disabled and with one chunk per batch, written by a process without access to the reference signals;
     (c) the join of predictions and references and all analyses, run once. After the run, every output table is checked against the manifests for missing backbone, arm, contrast or test-set rows. If rows are missing because of an execution error, the error is corrected without changing any analysis and stage (c) is repeated from its start; the repetition must reproduce every row of the first run byte for byte, and any difference is reported.
     Between stages (b) and (c) the predictions are used only for hashing, for the validity checks (defective checkpoints, item 2; window support, below) and for the comparison with the stored October predictions on MMPD-B (Section 3, Run) and, as a descriptive check, on the MCD-rPPG test half. If the comparison on MMPD-B gives a different HR readout for any window, the guarded inference of the affected recordings is repeated before stage (c). If the repetition does not reproduce the stage (b) predictions byte for byte, the inference set-up is corrected, stage (b) is repeated and both sets of hashes are reported. If it does, the difference is attributed to the October run, stage (c) uses the new predictions, and the number of windows with a different readout and the largest difference are reported. If, on either test set, more than 1% of the windows of any checkpoint have a different readout, stage (c) does not start until the cause has been established and reported.
     Window support. This fixes which checkpoints define the common support and does not change the window construction of Section 3. It follows the common-prediction-support rule of the original manuscript, which was defined over the models evaluated then. The confirmatory analyses use the windows with a valid prediction (a finite waveform that covers the whole window) from every confirmatory checkpoint; the number removed is reported. An exploratory contrast uses those of these windows that also have valid predictions from its own checkpoints, and a sensitivity analysis those that also have valid predictions in its own prediction set. A failed exploratory checkpoint therefore does not change a confirmatory result.
     Reruns. A rerun after a technical failure (a crash, an unreadable file or an out-of-memory error) repeats the failed stage from its committed inputs, must reproduce the hashes already committed for that stage, and is reported. An implementation error found in stage (c) after results have been seen is corrected in a new commit without changing a definition of Sections 3–5 or of the entries, and the results before and after the correction are both reported. Rows that the correction does not concern must be reproduced byte for byte.
     The person who runs stage (c), its start time and its log are recorded. No checkpoint, window, rule or metric is changed afterwards.
  5. Reporting scope, in addition to Sections 3–5 and the earlier entries: high-HR MAE for a reference HR of at least 120 bpm, with window and subject counts; low-HR MAE; MAE over all verified windows (on MCD-rPPG, the gap-free windows whose three reference estimates agree, whatever their HR); mean signed error, one-bin hit rate and half-HR rate by 10-bpm reference-HR bin, as in the original manuscript; per-subject results and the subgroup estimates of the original manuscript; recording-level scores in the benchmark convention of the original manuscript (one HR per recording from the whole predicted waveform and the whole contact PPG, narrow band; MAE, RMSE and Pearson correlation, and the MAE of the recordings whose reference is at least 100 bpm), as descriptive values; constant predictors, defined as the mean and the median reference HR of the development data of the same dataset (MMPD-A, on the windows selected by the rule of Section 3 for MMPD-B; the MCD-rPPG development half); the slope and rank correlation within high-HR windows, with intervals; within-subject change in HR on MCD-rPPG (MAE, slope, concordance correlation); the frame rate and key-frame interval of the MCD-rPPG test recordings; the key-frame-line fraction of every arm on MCD-rPPG; on MMPD-B, for every arm, the fraction of windows read within bins 33–35 or 50–52 among the windows whose reference lies outside these bins, for flagged and unflagged recordings (2026-11-11 entry); and, on MCD-rPPG, a common-window analysis in which a window read within 147.66–154.69 bpm by either arm of a contrast in a seed pair is removed from both arms of that pair. MCD-rPPG test recordings whose key-frame frequency (frame rate divided by key-frame interval) lies outside 147.66–154.69 bpm are analysed like the others, and their number is reported. The output-phase analysis is reported on development data only.
  6. Multiplicity. The confirmatory analyses are C1–C4 for three backbones on two test sets, 24 contrasts. Each is reported with its 95% interval, without correction for multiplicity, whatever its outcome. All other analyses are exploratory.
  7. Pre-written statements. These statements are in addition to the decision sentences of Section 4, and each names the test set on which it holds. For a contrast A − B on one test set the tiers are: T1, upper bound below 0, "A had a lower high-HR error than B", with the estimate (for C1, "reduced the high-HR error"); T2, point estimate below 0 and interval including 0, "in the same direction but inconclusive"; T3, point estimate at or above 0 and interval including 0, "did not reduce"; T4, lower bound above 0, "increased the high-HR error". For low-HR contrasts the tiers are read with "low-HR error". If a sensitivity analysis of Section 5 or of the entries puts a confirmatory contrast in another tier, the sentence gives both results.
     a. Improvement over no augmentation: C1, per backbone and test set, by tier. "All three backbones" is used only under the condition of Section 4 and then names MMPD-B. It is used for both test sets only if C1 is also in T1 for every backbone on MCD-rPPG; otherwise the MCD-rPPG tier of each backbone is given in the same sentence. A statement that aligned heart-rate range augmentation, without naming the method, reduced the high-HR error of a backbone on a test set requires T1 for C1, (i-pc) − (0) and (i-simple) − (0) there. Each statement from C1 is accompanied by the low-HR tier of the same contrast on the same test set; if that tier is T4, the sentence says that the low-HR error increased and gives the estimate.
     b. Alignment: C2 and C3, per backbone and test set, by tier; "alignment improved" is written only for T1, as in Section 4. C3 is stated as the effect of correcting the τ-rule shift of (i), not as an effect of alignment on time resampling in general. For RhythmFormer, if neither C2 nor C3 is in T1 on either test set, we state that alignment did not help RhythmFormer, provided that (i) − (i-simple) is not in T4 on either test set; if it is, we state instead that neither correction (C2, C3) helped RhythmFormer but that the τ-rule shift increased its error relative to resampling without a shift. If C2 or C3 is in T4 on MCD-rPPG, we add that it increased its error on MCD-rPPG, with the values under the two exclusion rules and on common windows, in addition to the statement of Section 5. The sentence of Section 5 is conditional on C2 only. In it, "attraction to the 2.5-Hz key-frame line" means a higher key-frame-line fraction as defined in Section 5; a causal statement about key frames is made only under rule 4 of docs/STAGE5_DEV_DIAGNOSTICS.md.
     c. Editing versus resampling: C4 and (ii-pc) − (i-simple), per backbone and test set, by tier. The word "superior" is used for a backbone only if C4 and (ii-pc) − (i-simple) are both in T1 on MMPD-B; this adds a condition to that of Section 4, because on development data (i-simple) had a lower error than (i-pc) for RhythmFormer. If C4 is in T1 on MMPD-B and (ii-pc) − (i-simple) is not, we state that pulse editing had a lower error than time resampling with the lag-preserving shift, and give (ii-pc) − (i-simple) by its tier. T1 on MCD-rPPG alone is stated as a lower error on MCD-rPPG. For T2 and T3 we use the sentence of Section 4 that no difference was established and give the interval; we do not write that the two performed alike. Amendment of Section 4, made before the Stage 5 test data are opened: its last decision sentence did not anticipate C4 in T4 on MMPD-B; in that case we state, instead of that sentence, that pulse editing had a higher high-HR error than time resampling with the lag-preserving shift.
     d. Mechanism: for PhysNet and FactorizePhys, we state that the rate-dependent misalignment alone increased the high-HR error on held-out data only if (ii-lag) − (ii-pc) is in T4 on MMPD-B for that backbone, and that it reproduced the loss of the original augmentation only if, in addition, C2 is in T1 on MMPD-B for that backbone. Otherwise the test values are given by tier and the development-data result is described as not confirmed on MMPD-B.
     e. Label distribution: we state that covering 80–100 bpm with edited labels was associated with a lower high-HR error only if, for PhysNet, (ii-pc-g80) − (ii-pc) and (ii-pc-g2) − (ii-pc-g) are both in T4 on MMPD-B and both have positive point estimates on MCD-rPPG. If only one of the two contrasts meets this condition, the statement is limited to that case. Otherwise no such statement is made. In every case the label-distribution results of the original manuscript are described as obtained with the misaligned editor. This rule replaces the development-data rule used during development for the statements of the paper; the development values are reported beside the test values, and a disagreement between them is stated.
     f. Processing, PhysNet: we state that the gain of (ii-pc) exceeded that of the identity edit only if (ii-pc) − (sham-pc) is in T1 on MMPD-B. For the high-HR contrast (sham-pc) − (0), T1 is stated as "part of the gain came from the editing process", an interval including 0 as "the identity edit alone did not change the high-HR error detectably", and T4 by tier. The low-HR contrast (sham-pc) − (0) is stated by tier. Whole-frame suppression: we state that restricting suppression and injection to forehead and cheek regions increased the high-HR error only if (roi-pc) − (ii-pc) is in T4 on MMPD-B; for T2 and T3 we state that a need for whole-frame suppression was not shown downstream.
     g. Tracking: for a backbone, we state that tracking within the high-HR range improved only if the rank correlation within high-HR windows of (ii-pc) minus that of (0) has a lower bound above 0 on both test sets; otherwise we state that the error fell without a demonstrated change in within-range tracking.
     h. Title: the title names an improvement by heart-rate range augmentation only if, for at least two backbones, C1, (i-pc) − (0) and (i-simple) − (0) each have an upper bound below 0 on both test sets; otherwise it describes a controlled study of when such augmentation helps. It names alignment or polarity only if C2 is in T1, or (ii-lag) − (ii-pc) is in T4, on MMPD-B for both PhysNet and FactorizePhys. "Pulse-rate editing" is used as a method name at the start of the title only if C4 and (ii-pc) − (i-simple) each have an upper bound below 0 on both test sets for at least two backbones.
     i. Abstract: the abstract gives the C1 estimates of the three backbones on both test sets in the primary band, whatever their tier, and says which intervals exclude 0; the intervals are given in the abstract if its word limit allows and otherwise in the text. It also gives, for the same backbones on MMPD-B, the tier of (ii-pc) − (i-simple), and names any backbone whose low-HR C1 is in T4 on MMPD-B.
     j. Background peaks on MMPD-B: we state that a confirmatory contrast on MMPD-B was sensitive to recordings with background periodicities only if the background-exclusion analysis of the 2026-11-11 entry puts that contrast in another tier and, for at least one of its two arms, the fraction of windows read within bins 33–35 or 50–52 (item 5) is at least 0.05 higher in flagged than in unflagged recordings (mean over seeds). Otherwise the fractions are reported without such a statement, beside the MMPD-A values.
  8. History. The paper states that the test sets were opened in four earlier stages of this project in October 2026; that the MCD-rPPG test half was part of the 192 recordings evaluated then, as was the development half, and how and when the halves were formed (Section 1 and the subject-list hashes of the manifest); that the test-set results of the original arms were known when the corrected arms were designed from an audit of the editor on PURE; that the corrected arms were evaluated on development data only; that on MMPD-A, 37 of the 760 verified windows used during development were included after a blinded manual review, a step that the rule of Section 3 for MMPD-B does not have, and that the development results are also given on the 723 windows selected by that rule; that some stored development predictions on MCD-rPPG had been computed while the GPU was short of memory, when the convolution library silently used a different computation plan, and those of nine runs on one recording with a different face box, chosen between two equally large detections; that the development results in the paper were recomputed from predictions made under the guards of item 4, and that the comparison tables (docs/dev_guard_comparison/, commit 19ecf5d) show no change of tier or of the outcome of a pre-specified rule, with the number of changed values and the largest change reported; that results of the earlier stages that are quoted from the October predictions were computed without these guards; that the development-data results of every arm in the manifests were known when this entry was committed; and that the results on the test sets are therefore a conditional confirmation.
